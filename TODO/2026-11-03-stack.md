# The stack: Dannan, Dagda, Lugh, Lir

Lir, the event system (`../lir`), joined the stack 2026-11-03 night on
Tom's say-so and takes its conventions whole; where this document says
"the three", Tom's rules apply to Lir alike.

What each of the three does alone, what they do together, and the seams
between them. Written 2026-11-03 night by the Dagda session, with the
Dannan and Lugh sessions' reviews folded in, from Tom's design ("Dannan:
Distributed Substrate Library Plan", rendered in
`../dannan/TODO/2026-11-02-dannan-plan.md`, cited as "plan" with line
numbers), the Decisions in `../dannan/TODO/ACTIVE.md`, Lugh's
`docs/architecture.md` and `TODO/ACTIVE.md`, and the three trees as they
stand. This file is the one copy; the other two projects point at it.
Each statement is marked:

- **Settled**: Tom's design or decision, with where it is written.
- **Proposed**: a recommendation for Tom to accept or amend. Nothing
  proposed is built until he does.

## Tom's rules for the stack (Settled, 2026-11-03 night)

1. **Dagda and Lugh use Dannan as their networking layer.** Neither has
   a transport, membership or node identity of its own.
2. **Lugh uses Dagda as its datastore**, for everything it persists,
   shared or node-local. Lugh has no store, keyring or sealing of its own.
3. **No code is duplicated between the three projects.** A piece needed
   by more than one lives in exactly one of them, and the others link it.
4. **The three use identical styles, conventions and layouts.** Dannan's
   are the reference (Tom to the Dagda session: "use all styles and
   conventions it uses"): one `.clang-format`, one header block form,
   one naming scheme, one branch and tag scheme, one set of rules in
   `CLAUDE.md` and `CONTRIBUTING.md`, one layout.

Tom gave rules 1 to 4 to all three sessions, 2026-11-03 night; Dannan
records them under Decisions in `../dannan/TODO/ACTIVE.md`. Everything below is written to these rules; where an earlier draft said
otherwise, it is gone.

## In one paragraph each

**Dannan** is the network, the virtual PHY and MAC. It makes a set of
trusted nodes, on any mix of home and rented machines and behind any NAT,
behave as one network that hosted systems transmit over. It owns
reachability, the two lanes, typed routes and channels, membership and
each node's capabilities and adverts, node identity, node key custody and
the scope check, placement as pure functions over the roster, work
classes and budgets, supervision and Status, and the shared foundations
every project above it uses (below). **It stores nothing** for a hosted
system and knows no key, path, user or merge. (Settled: plan, "What
Dannan is", 288; `ACTIVE.md` Principle 1.)

**Dagda** is the store: domains and their keys, sealing at rest,
immutable objects, tickets, claims and GC, the holdings trie and its
diff, heads (the opaque commit DAG whose merge the owner supplies),
node-local spaces, leases and claimed collections, per-origin counters,
retained values. It is a hosted system on Dannan, one peer among the
others, not a floor under them. **It never merges on anyone's behalf** and
gives no uniqueness, lock, transaction or linearizable write. (Settled:
plan 100-171, 284-289; Decisions, "Head stays in the core", "The store is
Dagda".)

**Lugh** is the directory and sign-on: people and services as principals,
groups, applications, factors, sessions and grants; two-factor sign-in;
OAuth 2 and OpenID Connect to applications; a management API, a dashboard
and read-only LDAP; token signing and the audit chain. It is a hosted
system on Dannan whose every persisted byte lives in Dagda under Lugh's
own codecs and merges, and **it is the source of every principal's
scope**. (Settled: plan 27, 193-197; rules 1 and 2.)

**Lir** is the event system: an MQTT broker exposing all of MQTT (5.0 and
3.1.1, every packet and property, QoS 0/1/2, retained, wills, wildcards,
shared subscriptions, persistent sessions), distributed, multimaster and
clustered, with Dannan as its network layer (Tom, 2026-11-03 night). It
links Dannan and Dagda, never Lugh, and persists everything in Dagda:
heads under its own merges, objects and claims for large payloads, leases
per shard, tickets for QoS acknowledgements. (Settled: Tom's requirement;
design "Lir: Distributed Event System on Dannan", rendered in
`../lir/TODO/2026-11-03-lir-plan.md`; Lir's findings against this
document in `../lir/TODO/ACTIVE.md`.)

## Layers

```
   applications, browsers, legacy clients        (HTTP, OIDC, LDAP: Lugh's)
   ----------------------------------------------------------------
   Lugh      Lir          Macha          AthenaSIP    (hosted systems: peers)
     | heads, node-local spaces, objects, leases, tickets, counters
   ----------------------------------------------------------------
   Dagda    domains, sealing | objects, claims, GC | heads | leases
     | routes, channels, placement, adverts, scope check, key custody
   ----------------------------------------------------------------
   Dannan   reach | lanes, routes, channels | membership | placement
            identity, key custody, scope | budgets | supervise | status
            foundations: crypto, logging, time, durable files, fixtures
   ----------------------------------------------------------------
   TCP/IP, NATs, the Internet
```

Dependencies point down only. Dannan links neither Dagda nor Lugh; Dagda
links Dannan, never Lugh; Lugh links Dannan and Dagda (Settled: plan 11,
13, 102). Where a lower layer needs something only a higher one knows, the
lower one declares an interface and the higher one implements it (scope,
below). Hosted systems never address one another (Settled: `ACTIVE.md`
Principle 4): Lugh reaches Macha or AthenaSIP only as an identity provider
reaches a relying party, by OAuth tokens over HTTP.

## The seams

### Dannan to every hosted system: `dannan::Context`

`dannan/context.h` is the whole contract (Settled; built, version 1):
Nodes, Routes, Channels, Identity, Budget, Faults, Time, Telemetry, and
status, events, readiness, activity, observations, threads, diagnostics.
Unstable until a tag says otherwise. The foundations (crypto, secure
memory, logging, time, durable files, fixtures) are linked beside it,
public headers of the `dannan` target.

### Durability per write (Settled, Tom, 2026-11-03 night)

Every Dagda write (objects, head commits, claims, node-local spaces)
carries a level the caller chooses (refined 2026-11-04):

- **Ephemeral**: received in memory and queued; returns at once; best
  effort, may be lost.
- **Persisted**: on disk on at least one node.
- **Replicated**: on disk on at least k nodes, k set per space (default
  2). Lir's QoS 1/2 and Lugh's credential changes and revocations.
- **Durable**: on disk on all available nodes of the replication set.
  Nodes Dannan already sees as dead are not waited on; a live node that
  does not answer is waited on to the deadline. Never undone; evidence
  names every node by Dannan node id as acknowledged, pending or refused,
  and the write's ticket reports again when pending nodes catch up.

Full text: `2026-11-03-macha-store-requirements.md`, section 8 head. For Lir's QoS
paths and Lugh's writes this flag is what they choose, in place of
waiting on a ticket or certificate by hand.

### Dagda to its hosted systems: `dagda::Context`

The plan's store nouns: Domains, Objects, Claims, Heads, Leases, Tickets,
Retained (Settled in shape: plan 215-221), plus node-local spaces. Dagda
designs it; it is built on Dannan's Context and handed to a hosted
system beside it. Head spaces take `replicas: N` at `open()` from the
start (Decision, 2026-11-03).

### Retained values: Dagda's, on Dannan's notify channels

Retained is a store noun (plan 221, "Store | Retained | retain(topic,
value)"; Level 1: "a notify channel whose last value also lands in a
head the hosted system owns"). Dagda builds `retain()` from Dannan's
notify channels (landed: `dannan/net/fanout.h`, `Context::channels()`,
Dannan `2f16981`) and a head of its own. Dannan never touches heads and
declares no seam for it; `ScopeSource` is the only interface of that kind
in the stack.

### Wire and formats for the store (Settled, Tom)

Dagda keeps Macha's store payload encodings byte for byte (packs, the tree
snapshot and delta forms, the Merkle tree over keys; on disk layout v19, by `dagda-migrate`),
carried inside Dannan's wire v1 framing under Dagda's own message numbers,
registered from 512. Protocol 23's framing, handshake and message numbers are gone
(Dannan's flag day); Dannan carries user payloads opaquely, so nothing
conflicts (the Dannan session).

### Node-local spaces (proposed; a requirement from Lugh)

Some state is one node's and must never leave it: Lugh's single-use
grants, a TOTP step on its owning node, Lir's sequence counter. Dagda gives it one
shape rather than each hosted system keeping a store:

- a head space opened with a node-local policy (this node only; never
  gossiped, offered, repaired or counted toward anyone's floor);
- sealed under a node-local domain whose key exists only on that node,
  sealed under Dannan's node key custody
  (`KeyCustody::derive("dagda/node-local/1")`), never wrapped to another
  node;
- the same codec, merge, read and `mutate_local` calls as any space, so
  a hosted system writes one kind of code for both;
- durable on the node with the same crash guarantees as a shared space.

### Scope: Lugh's data, Dannan's check, run by Dagda (proposed)

The plan gives Dannan `Identity::verify(principal, scope)` and
`scopes(principal)`, answered by "local read of the Lugh space when the
store is linked; cert-carried scope otherwise" (plan 210). Dannan cannot
link Lugh or Dagda, so:

- Dannan declares the interface: `contract::ScopeSource`, answering "may
  principal P write under prefix S?" from memory, never waiting, with
  evidence (the head generation it read).
- Lugh implements it over its scope head space and installs it in the
  node at start.
- Dagda's write-floor replicas call Dannan's `Identity::verify` before
  storing a commit or claim. Hosted systems that never touch the store
  (the event system, compute) call the same check.
- With no source installed (no Lugh on the cluster, or before Lugh
  exists), a write carried for the node's own principal or any node
  principal of the cluster is allowed and any other principal is refused.
  That is Macha today.
- The plan's fallback for a node without the Lugh space, a short-lived
  credential carrying its scope, is a second implementation of the same
  interface.
- Bootstrap: Lugh's own spaces are written by node principals, and
  genesis (`lugh init`, root) is their first commit.

### Which nodes hold Lugh's data (proposed)

Scope must be readable on every node that counts toward a write floor;
password hashes and second-factor secrets must not be. So Lugh's shared
state is two domains, and its node-local state a third kind:

- `lugh/scope`: principal identifiers, group memberships and scopes.
  Every node is a member, so every node can check a write.
- `lugh/secrets`: credentials (password hashes), second factors (TOTP
  secrets, recovery-code hashes), app passwords, signing-key material.
  Members are the nodes advertising the `lugh` capability; other nodes
  hold it blind for durability at most.
- Node-local spaces (single-use grants, TOTP steps where kept on the
  owning node): one per Lugh node, under that node's node-local domain.
  Sessions are replicated with `lugh/secrets` (Tom, 2026-11-04).

Domain keys are wrapped to node certificates (plan 122); node-local
domain keys are under Dannan's node key custody. Lugh holds no key at
rest of its own; its token signing keys are records in `lugh/secrets`,
and it may seal fields inside its state for depth with Dannan's AEAD.
A Macha-only node can check scope and can never read a password hash.

### One process per node (proposed)

A node is one process running Dannan. A product binary (Macha, Lugh,
AthenaSIP) links the stack and is a node; several nodes of one cluster
may share a machine, each with its own state path and port, and each
advertises what it hosts (`lugh`, `lir`, `macha`, `athena`). Lugh then runs as
its own user and process, as its security design requires. Dannan's
supervisor still loads plugins for systems that want to share a node.

### Revoking a node (proposed, for Dannan's step 10)

Lugh needs a removed node refused at once; the plan only rotates (plan
291). A revocation is a record signed by the cluster CA naming a node id
and certificate serial, gossiped and persisted like an identity reset,
and grow-only. Every node holding it refuses that certificate at the
handshake and closes its sessions with that node at once: everywhere
within one gossip round, immediately on the issuing node.

### Async shape

One rule and one shape across the stack: every operation takes a
deadline and the caller's executor and posts its completion there, by
callback, never inline (Settled; built in Dannan). Dagda's Context
follows it. Lugh takes the same shape, with no awaitable adapter (Tom,
in the Lugh session); AthenaSIP is already on a strand with callbacks.

### Versions and message numbers

Each library's Context is versioned from 1; a hosted system reports the
version it was built against and a mismatch is refused (Settled: Dannan
`CLAUDE.md`). Until Dannan has a tag carrying the Context, a consumer
pins a commit: Lugh links Dannan now from `../dannan` at
`feature/wire-v1` `785e393` (Tom, in the Lugh session), and moves to a
tag when one exists. Thereafter Dagda pins a Dannan tag, and Lugh pins a
Dagda tag and the Dannan tag that Dagda pins; Lir pins a Dannan tag and
a Dagda tag. Dannan 0.2.0 is the first tag carrying the Context (Tom,
2026-11-04). Message numbers: free registration from 512 (Decided,
item 4).

### Lir on Dagda (proposed)

Lir persists everything in Dagda (rule 2). Its uses, as the Lir session
gives them, and how Dagda's design carries each:

- Leases `lir/shard/<n>` (256 shards by default), with epochs, as the
  per-shard fan-out coordinator: the lease template (plan 161-165).
- Objects and claims for payloads over 64 KiB (plan 126-136).
- Durability per QoS path (the Lir session): QoS 0 waits on nothing;
  QoS 1/2 inline waits on the acceptance certificate (plan 145) of the
  inflight commit holding the message; QoS 1/2 out of line waits on the
  object's ticket (plan 132), then makes the commit naming it and waits
  on its certificate, which is Macha's extent rule; retained waits on the
  retained commit's certificate for QoS 1/2 and on local acceptance for
  QoS 0.
- Spaces per shard, records as keys (plan 149): `lir/retained/<n>`,
  `lir/inflight/<n>`, `lir/sessions/<n>` for n below S (256 by default,
  64 workable), each `replicas: 3`; each client id and each topic is a
  key. 3 x S spaces is within Dagda's design, which sets no bound; a
  bound, if any, comes from Dagda's budgets.
- Inflight: one commit per shard per tick (Lir already batches), each
  dot a Merkle key, so a commit rewrites a few leaves; only
  acknowledgement state is in the head. PUBACK latency is the tick plus
  one certificate round. The rate goes to Tom (Lir's budgets section is
  unwritten).
- No wildcard state in Dagda: the global wildcard index is soft state in
  Lir's memory, rebuilt from announcements over Lir's channel (Lir's
  design).
- The per-origin `seq`: Lir's dot is (origin, seq), seq monotonic
  across restarts. On start Lir sets seq to one more than the larger of
  the value persisted in its node-local space and the highest seq for
  its own origin in any head it holds or fetches. Dagda provides the
  node-local space; not a per-origin counter, which is merged by sum
  across nodes. Dannan's `durability_epoch` rides in Lir's envelope for
  dedup and diagnosis only: it is a random 128-bit id fresh in every
  process (Dannan session), telling a restart from a reconnect but never
  which incarnation is newer, so nothing is fenced by it.
- Placement alignment: a shard's space replicas and its lease holder
  must be the same nodes. *Recommended:* `open()` takes the owner's
  placement salt (a Dannan `placement::Scheme`, which already has no
  default salts and lets the caller name them), so Lir computes the same
  replica set Dagda uses; and the Heads noun also returns a space's
  replica set as evidence, so an owner can check rather than assume.
- Items 4, 5 and 7 change Lir's design and go to Tom; nothing here is
  built on them until he settles them (the Lir session).
- (2026-11-04, Tom to the Lir session) Lir's layout is approved:

## Security requirements Lugh puts on the stack

Raised by the Lugh session, 2026-11-03 night. Each names who must meet it.

a. **Binding and no rollback** (Dagda). A sealed value is bound by its
   AEAD associated data to its domain and its object id (plan 116 binds
   the ciphertext digest to the plaintext id; the domain must be in the
   binding too), so it cannot be moved, reordered or replayed under
   another domain or id. A head space never moves back: commits are
   content-identified with parents (plan 144), so a commit already an
   ancestor of the held head set changes nothing, and an unrelated one
   is a divergence handed to the owner's merge, never a replacement
   (plan 146). Resurrecting a revoked credential through a merge is
   prevented by Lugh's grow-only tombstones. *Proposed as a Dagda
   requirement, with a test for each, for shared and node-local spaces
   alike.* A node writing a fresh commit that restores old content is a
   lying node, outside the trust boundary (f).
b. **Node-local durable state** (Dagda). Single-use grants are one
   node's, durable and sealed on it, never replicated: Dagda's
   node-local spaces (above). Sessions are replicated instead (Tom,
   2026-11-04).
c. **TOTP replay** (Lugh, on Dannan or Dagda). A code can be presented at
   any node. Either the check is forwarded to one owning node per user
   (Dannan placement, failing closed) with the step in that node's
   node-local space, or the step is guarded by a per-user Dagda lease.
   Never replicated state. Open; Lugh's to design.
d. **The partition bound** (stated, accepted). Revocation by credential
   version, disabling an account, and the scope fallback all take effect
   on a node only when the head, or the credential's expiry, reaches it.
   A partitioned node honours a revoked session, a disabled account or a
   withdrawn scope until then. Lugh bounds it by keeping access tokens
   and fallback credentials short-lived.
e. **Uniqueness by name** (Lugh's merge). Two nodes can both create
   "alice" in a partition. Tokens carry the immutable principal id, so
   OAuth is safe; sign-in and LDAP bind are by name. Lugh's merge
   resolves duplicates deterministically, and Lugh refuses sign-in by an
   ambiguous name until it has.
f. **The trust boundary** (stated, accepted). Any node holding a cluster
   certificate may write Lugh's spaces, so one compromised node can
   rewrite the directory. That is inside the stack's non-Byzantine limit
   (plan 291) and is the accepted boundary. It makes removing a node
   urgent.
g. **Key custody** (Dannan). Lugh's master key today never exists in
   clear at rest: a TPM-sealed systemd credential in locked memory. With
   Lugh holding no keys, that standard moves to Dannan's node key
   custody: the node certificate's private key and the root that
   node-local domain keys are sealed under are sealed credentials, in
   locked memory, never on disk in clear.
h. **The audit chain** (Lugh, on Dagda). One hash chain per node: a head
   space per node, append-only by Lugh's merge, or a node-local space
   with export. Lugh's choice.

## Decided (Tom, 2026-11-04)

Settled in the Dagda session's walkthrough and, the same day, in the
Dannan session (reported by it); they replace the proposals above where
the text below still says "proposed":

1. **Shared code** lives in the lowest project that uses it; the others
   link it (the reading of rule 3).
2. **One process per node**; each product binary links the stack, is a
   node and advertises what it hosts.
3. **Dannan 0.2.0** with CMake install and export targets
   (`find_package(dannan 0.2 CONFIG)`, `dannan::dannan`; Dannan
   `295ca4b`); the tag waits only on the pre-release sanitizer run.
   Consumers pin it once cut.
4. **Message numbers**: Dannan's core types are 1 to 511; user types
   start at 512 and are registered freely, no per-project blocks, a
   clash refused at registration (enforced in Dannan's code). Replaces
   the proposed blocks.
5. **Scope seam**: Dannan declares `contract::ScopeSource` and
   `Identity::verify`; Lugh installs the source; Dagda's write-floor
   replicas, and every other hosted system, check through it. The
   no-source default and the bootstrap stand as written.
6. **Lugh's two domains**: `lugh/scope` (every node a member),
   `lugh/secrets` (Lugh nodes members, others blind at most).
7. **Node revocation**: a CA-signed, grow-only revocation record, in
   Dannan's step 10.
8. **Authentication in Lir is pluggable**, "like everywhere else"; a
   Lugh plugin depends on Lugh. Token verification does **not** move
   into Dannan.
9. **HTTP stays each hosted system's own**; there is no shared HTTPS
   stack.
10. **Presets**: none, as Dannan has; **Lugh is the exception**, because
    of auth, and keeps its presets.
11. **Process-level tests: GoogleTest only, and no Python** in any of
    the projects.
12. **Binding and no rollback** (requirement a) are Dagda requirements:
    a value is bound to its domain and its id, a space never rolls back,
    each with a test. Macha's existing single domain keeps its current
    bytes (format compatibility).
13. **The stated limits are accepted**: the partition bound (d) and the
    trust boundary (f).
14. **Node-local spaces** are a Dagda feature (above).
15. **Lugh's sessions are replicated**, not node-local, so a user's
    session works at any Lugh node. They hold secrets, so they belong
    with `lugh/secrets` (Lugh nodes only); the exact space is Lugh's
    design. Single-use grants and TOTP steps are unchanged (node-local or
    a lease; Lugh's design).
16. **Kept-forever tombstones** are Lugh's merge rule; Dagda's template
    keeps its retention window.
17. **ETags**: Lugh's version field checked inside `mutate_local`.
18. **No `lir.local`**: other projects that want the bus are ordinary
    MQTT clients of Lir, through its pluggable authentication.
19. **Lir's multimaster** is as described: any node accepts any client
    operation, the per-shard lease is a fan-out coordinator, and both
    sides of a split serve and merge after.
20. **Placement alignment**: `open()` takes the owner's placement salt,
    and Dagda reports each space's replica set as evidence.
21. **Lir's own storage layout** is Lir's to bring to Tom.

Also settled for Dagda alone (requirements document, section 8 head):
four durability levels per write (Ephemeral, Persisted, Replicated with
